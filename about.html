Communication foundation

Built through content delivery.
Strengthened by multi-channel work.

My communications background started at The Starter Labs in Mumbai, producing 60 to 100 content assets every month across seven FMCG and retail brands. Email, social, web, and paid media, running in parallel across multiple client accounts and deadlines.

That environment grounded me early in something that has stayed consistent across every role since: volume does not excuse inconsistency. If the message drifts between the brief and the published content, the audience notices. Keeping tone aligned, maintaining quality under pressure, and catching errors before they go out became habits I still rely on.

I moved to London to study Strategic Advertising and Marketing Communications at the University of Greenwich, which gave me the framework to apply that practical experience with more intention and structure.

What the work taught me

Most communication problems
are not writing problems.
They are structure problems.

At TEDx Greenwich I coordinated communications across a public event series built around a 200+ attendee TEDx live event. Speakers, sponsors, university stakeholders, TED brand teams, external partners, all needing different things, at different times, with no room for a message to go out wrong.

What I learned from that: the writing is rarely where things break. Things break in the gaps. The brief that never got translated. The update that went to the wrong person. The approval that stalled because no one owned it. I started thinking less about how to write the message and more about how to build the system that gets it there.

At Good Nugget I took a neurodivergent-led organisation through a full communication repositioning, from B2C to B2B, from 400-word mission statements to 40-word ones, from scattered channel activity to a structured content plan. Engagement improved. The sharper outcome was clarity on what it was saying, who it was for and why it mattered.

At Pangea Café, the challenge was different: turning a social issue into a community-facing campaign concept. The work used coffee as a familiar entry point into anti-racism communication, building a campaign around belonging, cultural connection and participation rather than only awareness.

How I work

Clear on the message.
Organised in delivery.

I work well in fast-moving environments with multiple stakeholders, timelines, and priorities. I'm used to managing approvals, keeping communication structured, and making sure the right information reaches the right audience clearly and on time.

What I am building towards

The best communication
does more than explain.
It changes something.

The M+C Saatchi Open House is a selective industry programme. Getting in required a pitch. Staying engaged has required applying advertising and campaign thinking to live briefs from real clients, including Bundesliga. It has sharpened how I think about earned attention, about what makes a message stick, and about the gap between communication that is technically correct and communication that actually moves someone.

That thinking belongs in places where the audience is real, the stakes matter, and communication has to do more than fill a channel. Higher education, charities, public bodies and membership organisations all depend on trust, clarity and participation. Good communication in those spaces does not just explain what is happening. It helps people understand why it matters and what to do next.
