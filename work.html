Event communications Stakeholder coordination Sponsor outreach Approval tracking Post-event reporting

Project brief

Build a clear communications flow for a public-facing TEDx event with multiple audiences, moving details and fixed deadlines.

The event needed communication that could hold together across speaker updates, sponsor outreach, partner conversations, audience-facing content and internal approvals. The challenge was not just writing the message. It was making sure the right version reached the right people at the right time.

Key KPIs

200+
attendees supported through live event communications
15 - 25
external organisations approached through sponsor and partner outreach
5 - 8
conversations progressed into active sponsor or partner workflows

The gap

The event had one public face, but many moving parts behind it.

Speakers, sponsors, partners, university teams and audience touchpoints all needed different information at different times. Without a clear communication rhythm, details could easily slip, duplicate or reach the wrong person.

What I noticed

Most of the risk sat between people, not inside the copy.

The writing mattered, but the bigger issue was coordination: who needed an update, who owned the approval, what version was final and when each message needed to go out.

The approach

Create one communications plan across audience, speaker, sponsor and internal updates.

I used shared planning, clearer message routes and approval tracking to keep public-facing content, stakeholder updates and post-event reporting aligned through the event cycle.

Process

How I kept the communication moving.

Before the event

Mapped speakers, sponsors, partners, and university stakeholders. Built a shared plan across web, social, email, and speaker updates. Ran sponsor outreach across 15 to 25 organisations.

During the event

Kept event information, stakeholder coordination, and audience-facing updates moving through clear approval routes aligned with TEDx expectations.

After the event

Closed the loop with post-event reporting, partner follow-up, and planning inputs for the next cycle. Five to eight organisations progressed into active sponsor or partner workflows.

Final result

A clearer communications system for a live event with multiple audiences and approval points.

The work helped keep public updates, speaker information, sponsor outreach and internal coordination moving through one clearer system. That mattered because the event had several audiences, fixed deadlines and little room for confusion once information went public.

The result was not just more content. It was a more reliable way to plan, approve and deliver communication across the event cycle.

This project demonstrates
Public sector campaign planning Audience and inclusion Multi-site delivery Evaluation framework

Project brief

Design a borough-wide Christmas events campaign that works across three town centres and multiple resident groups.

The brief asked for a public events programme that could bring residents together safely across Basildon, Billericay and Wickford. The campaign needed to balance celebration, inclusion, local business support, sponsorship income and post-campaign evaluation.

Key KPIs

6+
public-facing events proposed across three town centres
80%
resident satisfaction target built into evaluation
20 - 30%
target income offset through sponsorship and local support

The gap

A borough-wide campaign could not rely on one audience, one channel or one town-centre experience.

The campaign needed to serve families, older residents, young people, diverse communities, residents with limited digital access and local businesses. Without a clear audience and delivery structure, the programme risked becoming fragmented across locations, channels and resident needs.

What I noticed

Inclusive public events need access planned in from the start.

Different resident groups needed different routes into the campaign. Some would respond through digital channels, some through community spaces, some through local businesses and some through physical visibility across the town centres. Inclusion could not sit at the end of the plan. It had to shape the programme from the beginning.

The approach

Build one campaign framework with audience, channel, governance and evaluation built in.

I structured the proposal around a six-strand events programme, supported by a multi-channel communications plan, a delivery timeline, governance checkpoints and a post-campaign evaluation framework tied to resident satisfaction, access and budget awareness.

Process

How I built the proposal.

Before launch

Mapped six audience groups and structured the campaign across Basildon, Billericay, and Wickford. Built a six-strand events programme with channel planning and stakeholder preparation.

During campaign

Connected town-centre events, community activity, and resident-facing communications through a multi-channel plan with launch and live-event messaging.

After campaign

Built evaluation into the timeline: resident feedback, satisfaction review, budget awareness, and governance checkpoints for future delivery.

Final result

A borough-ready campaign proposal with audience access, delivery structure and evaluation built in.

The proposal turned a broad public events brief into a practical campaign framework. It showed how events, communications, sponsorship, inclusion, governance and evaluation could work together across three town centres.

The value of the work was in making the campaign deliverable: clear audiences, clear channels, clear delivery checkpoints and a way to measure whether the programme had worked for residents.

Project type Brand strategy and communications repositioning Audience HR Directors · EDI Officers · Corporate teams · Neurodivergent communities My input Audience research · Tone of voice · Positioning · Visual identity direction · Social media strategy

Project brief

Reposition NDSA’s communication for HR and EDI audiences without losing the lived-experience foundation.

The organisation needed to speak more clearly to HR Directors, EDI officers and corporate audiences while staying rooted in neurodivergent lived experience. The existing communication carried strong purpose, but the message was too dense, too internal and too difficult for new audiences to quickly act on. The work was to simplify the tone, sharpen the positioning and build a clearer communication system across brand, content and social media.

Key KPIs

12%
engagement improvement across priority social channels
400 to 40
mission statement simplified while keeping the core meaning
3
core deliverables structured: tone of voice, visual identity and social media strategy

The gap

The organisation had a meaningful offer, but the message was too complex for the audience it needed to reach.

The existing communication was built around advocacy language and internal understanding. It explained the mission in detail, but it did not make the value easy enough for HR and EDI audiences to understand quickly. The tone was losing the message through over-complicated and saturated information.

What I noticed

Audience repositioning needed more than shorter copy. It needed a clearer reason to care.

The audience needed simplicity, relevance and motivation. HR and EDI decision-makers did not just need neurodiversity education. They needed to understand the workplace problem, the value of NDSA’s expertise and the practical next step.

The approach

Build a clearer brand communication system across message, tone, visual identity and social content.

I reframed the communication around audience need: clearer language, a sharper positioning route, a more supportive tone of voice, stronger visual direction and a social media strategy designed to educate, engage and convert HR and corporate audiences.

Process

How I rebuilt the communication system.

Audience and insight

Defined HR Directors, EDI officers, and corporate audiences. Used research to identify the need for simplicity, relevance, and motivation.

Message and tone

Simplified the mission statement from around 400 words to 40. Reworked tone from over-complicated to clear, actionable, and inclusive.

Channels and identity

Developed positioning, visual identity direction, and a social strategy across LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and email campaigns.

Before / after message
BeforeOver-complicated, saturated information that required too much effort from a new audience.
AfterClear, actionable and inclusive communication that made the value easier to understand.

Motto shiftFrom “Nothing about us, without us” feeling unclear and demanding in this context, to “With NDSA, it’s not just about inclusion - it’s about transformation.”

Final result

A clearer brand communication system that made the offer easier to understand, position and communicate.

The work moved the organisation from dense, internally familiar communication to a clearer audience-led brand system. The repositioning clarified who the message was for, what value NDSA offered and how the organisation could show up across tone of voice, visual identity and social content.

Engagement improved by 12%, but the more useful shift was structural: the organisation had a sharper answer to what it was saying, who it was for and why it mattered.

Project type Community engagement campaign concept proposal Campaign context Anti-racism café campaign · Immigrant-owned business · Community connection Audience Socially conscious coffee lovers · Local café visitors · Diverse communities · People who value inclusive spaces My input Audience insight · Campaign strategy · Big idea · Rollout plan · Poster direction · Activation ideas Campaign idea #BrewedToUnite: Welcome Beyond Borders

Project brief

Create a community engagement campaign that positions the café as a space for connection, belonging and anti-racism.

The brief needed a campaign idea that could respond to rising racism and xenophobia while staying rooted in what the café naturally represents: global ingredients, immigrant ownership, everyday gathering and cultural connection. The concept used coffee as a shared ritual to turn a social issue into a community-facing campaign.

Key KPIs

1
community engagement campaign concept built around anti-racism and belonging
3
rollout phases planned across tease, launch and sustain
4
engagement routes proposed: posters, in-café activation, pop-ups and Instagram series

The gap

The café had the ingredients of a community space, but the campaign needed to make that role visible.

The café already carried a strong story through its immigrant ownership, global ingredients and multicultural roots. The challenge was to turn that story into a public-facing campaign that could speak against racism without becoming heavy, generic or disconnected from the café experience.

What I noticed

Coffee crosses borders before it reaches the cup.

The strongest insight was that coffee already carries the campaign message. It moves across countries, cultures, people and rituals. That made it a natural symbol for connection, giving the campaign a way to talk about belonging through something familiar, everyday and shared.

The approach

Use coffee as the bridge between anti-racism communication and community participation.

I built the campaign around #BrewedToUnite: Welcome Beyond Borders. The idea translated the café's multicultural story into posters, in-café participation, pop-up conversations, community storytelling and social content that could invite people into the message instead of only asking them to observe it.

Process

How I built the campaign concept.

01 Defined the social problem: racism, xenophobia and the feeling of alienation experienced by immigrant and diverse communities.
02 Mapped the audience: socially conscious coffee lovers, diverse local communities and people who value inclusive everyday spaces.
03 Built the core insight around coffee as a universal connector across cultures, borders and conversations.
04 Created the campaign idea: #BrewedToUnite: Welcome Beyond Borders.
05 Planned the campaign rollout across tease, launch and sustain, including posters, in-café activation, pop-ups, Instagram content and community storytelling.
Campaign rollout
TeaseCryptic posters, social teasers and café cues asking whether a cup of coffee can break barriers.
LaunchCampaign posters, Unity Wall, Unity Brew concept and public-facing anti-racism messaging inside and outside the café.
SustainBrewing Tales sessions, Community Spotlights, mascot-led anti-hate Instagram series and pop-up Coffee for Connection stalls.
Campaign line #BrewedToUnite: Welcome Beyond Borders Core thought Coffee connects people across borders, cultures and everyday rituals. Public message At Pangea, every cup tells a story.

Final result

A community engagement campaign concept that turned coffee into a symbol of connection.

The concept turned the café's multicultural roots into a campaign platform for anti-racism, belonging and community participation. Instead of treating the café as only a place to buy coffee, the campaign positioned it as a space where people, cultures and stories could meet.

The value of the concept was in building a campaign that could move across message, space and participation: from posters and social content to in-café activations, pop-ups and community storytelling.

Project brief

Create a consistent brand voice and launch communication system for a homegrown premium brand.

The brand needed a communications system that could introduce a homegrown premium vodka brand with a clear Mumbai-rooted story. The work had to connect the name, product quality, local origin and launch idea into one consistent voice across the copy guide, social content, launch video direction and website copy.

Key KPIs

1
copy guide created to define Salsette’s tone, vocabulary and writing rules
3
launch communication streams supported: pre-launch, launch and product feature content
1
website copy framework developed for the public-facing brand site

The gap

The brand needed to feel premium, homegrown and consistent before launch.

The brand had a strong origin story and product proposition, but the communication needed a clear voice. The challenge was to avoid generic spirits language and build a brand world that connected Mumbai, smoothness, local sourcing and premium lifestyle cues without losing consistency across channels.

What I noticed

The strongest route was not just product description. It was the idea of where the moment starts.

The name carried the heritage of Bombay and the energy of Mumbai. That gave the brand a stronger platform than product features alone. The communication needed to make #StartWithSalsette feel like a brand thought, not just a hashtag.

The approach

Build the launch communication around origin, tone and repeatable brand language.

I supported the brand's communication strategy through a copy guide, tone of voice framework, launch campaign copy, storyboard feedback and website copy. The goal was to make every audience-facing touchpoint sound like one consistent brand, from launch content to the live website.

Process

How I built the brand communication system.

Foundation

Defined what the brand is, how it is made, and where it sits in the market. Built tone of voice guidance around homegrown pride and premium-but-approachable language.

Pre-launch and launch

Created writing rules and launch copy across grid concepts, #StartWithSalsette messaging, and product feature posts.

Live channels

Developed website copy for the brand story, USP sections, and cocktail recipe content so every touchpoint sounded like one brand.

Brand voice Energetic, fun, sophisticated and homegrown.
Sacred words
Premium · Spirit · Homegrown · Smooth · Classic · Distinguished · Experimental · Lively
Writing rules
Short headers, sentence case, British English and consistent #StartWithSalsette usage.

Final result

A consistent launch communication system for a homegrown premium brand.

The work turned the brand's origin, product features and Mumbai-rooted story into a connected brand communication system. The copy guide gave the brand a reusable voice, the launch content created a route for #StartWithSalsette, and the website copy translated the brand world into public-facing sections.

The outcome was not just individual copy assets. It was a strategic communication foundation that could support launch, social content, product education and the live website.
